Hitting my 50th Munro felt like a massive milestone, and Beinn Mhanach was the perfect peak to mark the occasion. It wasn't just about the number, though—this was my first true winter Munro.

I had great company for the day with Joa. The journey started with a bit of a "slog"—about two hours of walking just to reach the base of the actual ascent. It’s a beautiful, winding walk through the glen, but it definitely tests your patience before the real climbing begins. We had to navigate six river crossings on the way in; luckily, the water was only about ankle-deep, but it kept us focused.

Once we started heading up, the fun really began. The climb was steep, and as we gained height, the conditions turned into a proper winter wonderland. This was the first time I really had to rely on my winter kit—the ice axe and crampons were absolute lifesavers.

Around the 700m–800m mark, the snow line became serious. By the time we reached the plateau and the summit, we were pushing through waist-deep snow. Everything was pure white, completely silent, and incredibly beautiful. There’s no feeling quite like standing at the top of your 50th peak in conditions like that.

Note to self: Always check the MWIS (Mountain Weather Information Service) before tackling this one in the colder months. The snow gets deep fast up there!
